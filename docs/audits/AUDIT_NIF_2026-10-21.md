## Findings

### LOW

#### NIF-D5-2026-10-21-01: FO76 and Starfield `bhkRagdollSystem` payloads are Havok tagfiles (`TAG0`), not packfiles, so `extract_ragdoll` logs a misleading "packfile: magic mismatch" WARN on every import of 820 vanilla NIFs, and `physal.md` claims the container is decoded for them
- **Severity**: LOW
- **Dimension**: Collision/Shader Parsing
- **Game Affected**: FO76 (`bsver` 155), Starfield (`bsver` ≥ 172)
- **Location**: `crates/nif/src/import/collision/ragdoll.rs:361-374` (`extract_np_ragdoll`), `crates/nif/src/import/mod.rs:163` (called from every `import_nif_scene*`), `crates/nif/src/blocks/collision/havok_packfile.rs:377-384`, `docs/engine/physal.md:189` and `:318-325`
- **Status**: NEW (searched open and closed issues for `TAG0`, `tagfile`, `bhkRagdollSystem Starfield` and `extract_np_ragdoll`: no match; #2355 counted the 316 Starfield `bhkRagdollSystem` blocks but never identified their format)
- **Description**: `extract_ragdoll` runs on every scene import. When a scene has no discrete `bhkConstraint`, it falls through to `extract_np_ragdoll`, which hands the first `bhkRagdollSystem` blob to `decode_np_ragdoll` and then `parse_havok_packfile`. Fallout 4 blobs are classic packfiles. Every FO76 and Starfield blob instead starts with a big-endian size word followed by the `TAG0` tag (`00 00 4a 9c 54 41 47 30 …`), which is the Havok tagfile container. `parse_havok_packfile` rejects them on the magic, and `extract_np_ragdoll` logs `log::warn!("extract_np_ragdoll: bhkRagdollSystem block {i} not decoded: havok packfile: magic mismatch")`.
- **Evidence**: The `ragdecode` probe ran over `SeventySix - Meshes.ba2` + `MeshesExtra.ba2` and Starfield `Meshes01/02/Patch`:
  ```
  [fo76] {"err havok packfile: magic mismatch": 249} files 249 imported 0
    meshes\actors\robot\parts\handrightcannon.nif: … (len 11660 magic [00, 00, 2d, 8c, 54, 41, 47, 30])
  [sf] {"err havok packfile: magic mismatch": 571} files 571 imported 0
    meshes\actors\hoppera\characterassets\skeleton_ref.nif: … (len 74720 magic [00, 01, 23, e0, 54, 41, 47, 30])
  ```
  `physal.md:189` puts FO4, FO76 and Starfield in one row whose status is "container + object table decoded (#3809, `parse_havok_packfile`)". `:318-325` describes "the classic packfile header" as *the* FO4+ container. The `np_ragdoll.rs` module doc also opens with "FO4+ `bhkRagdollSystem` payload".
- **Impact**: Nothing breaks. FO76 and Starfield ragdolls are out of scope, and `None` is the right result. However, each distinct FO76 or Starfield actor model that gets imported logs a WARN, including robot hand parts. The WARN names a "magic mismatch", which reads like corrupt data rather than a known unsupported format. The doc row also tells the next reader that the container is decoded for those two games, when the decoder in fact rejects 100% of their blobs. Whoever picks up FO76 or Starfield ragdolls will start from the wrong premise.
- **Related**: #5015 (FO4 decode), #3809 (container), #2355.
- **Suggested Fix**: Recognise the `TAG0` tag (offset 4) in `parse_havok_packfile` and give it a distinct error kind or variant, or check it in `extract_np_ragdoll`. Decline that case at `debug` level with "Havok tagfile (FO76/Starfield) — not decoded". Split the `physal.md` row so FO4 says "packfile" and FO76/Starfield say "tagfile, opaque". Change the module doc to "FO4".

- **Game Affected**: FO4 (precombine CSG path); every title with object, terrain or placement LOD (LOD importers)

- **Description**: The skill's Dim 1 contract says every consumer that caches a scene passes it through `note_scene_health`. `bbb4dc3d7` wired that into the REFR loader, the pre-parse worker and the NPC loader (`references/import.rs:57`, `streaming/pre_parse.rs:290`, `scene/nif_loader.rs:335`). #### NIF-D5-2026-10-21-02: `extract_np_ragdoll` re-implements NIF world-transform composition (`node_world`) beside `compose_transforms`, and walks parents through a bare `NiNode` downcast instead of `as_ni_node`
- **Severity**: LOW
- **Dimension**: Collision/Shader Parsing (ragdoll import)
- **Game Affected**: FO4 (`bhkRagdollSystem` path only)
- **Location**: `crates/nif/src/import/collision/ragdoll.rs:531-579` (`node_parents`, `node_world`); compare `crates/nif/src/import/transform.rs:25` (`compose_transforms`) and `crates/nif/src/import/walk/mod.rs:102` (`as_ni_node`)
- **Status**: NEW
- **Description**: `node_world` builds a node's rest world transform by hand-composing the `NiTransform` chain (row-to-column `Mat3`, `t += r * (local.t * s)`, `r *= lr`, `s *= local.scale`). This is the same math as `compose_transforms`, but without the #4549 overflow sanitiser. `body_offset` rejects non-finite results afterwards, so nothing unsafe gets through. Both `node_parents` and `node_world` recognise a parent only through `downcast_ref::<NiNode>()`. A bone under any NiNode subclass with its own struct would stop the chain early: `BsMultiBoundNode`, `NiSwitchNode`, `BsOrderedNode`, `BsValueNode`, `NiBillboardNode`, `BsRangeNode`, `BsWeakReferenceNode`, `BsFaceGenNiNode`. A truncated chain gives a wrong rest world transform, and the body is then posed off its bone with an `is_t` offset computed from that wrong transform.
- **Evidence**: The `census` probe found 0 non-`NiNode` node structs in all 75 FO4 skeletons that carry a `bhkRagdollSystem`, so vanilla is unaffected. The `as_ni_node` doc (`walk/mod.rs:91-101`) exists to stop exactly this hand-rolled downcast ("Walkers should call this instead of hand-rolling the downcast chain").
- **Impact**: None on vanilla content. The cost is a second copy of transform composition (the global "improve existing code rather than duplicating logic" rule) and a latent mis-pose on modded skeletons.
- **Related**: #5015, #4549, #142/#148.
- **Suggested Fix**: Compose with `compose_transforms` over the parent chain and convert once to `Mat3` at the end. Build the parent map with `as_ni_node` (widen it to `pub(crate)`) and also handle the `switch_active_children` types.

## Verified, not reported

- **#6066 motion-index read (probe `main`)**: in all 75 FO4 ragdoll blobs, the material (`+16`), motion-property (`+32`), motion (`+48`) and body (`+64`) arrays have equal counts. On 986 of 986 bodies, the body row's `u16` at `+18` equals the motion index, and so does the motion row's `u16` at `+0`. The body index equals the motion index on only 212 bodies, so the decoder is right to use the motion index. These two `u16` fields look like explicit per-row ids. On vanilla, reading them would give exactly what the decoder reads now. I record this as a possible hardening point for modded rigs that share material rows; I am not filing it, because no vanilla content can tell the two readings apart.
- **#5960 motor tail**: the bare and malleable arms consume the motor once. The breakable wrapper's own `consume_motor` is still the only table, and `Strength` is skipped only for decoded kinds. Constraint drift is 0 across 8 types.
- **#5737**: `parse_shader_type_data_fo4` overrides only types 1 and 5 and delegates the rest. The gates match nif.xml (`#NI_BS_LTE_FO4#` = `bsver <= 139`, `#BS_FO4_2#` = `130..=139`, nif.xml lines 6619-6636).
- **#6130 `HAIR`**: `fo3nv_f1::HAIR = 0x0004_0000` matches nif.xml `BSShaderFlags` bit 18 "Hair" (nif.xml line 6168). It is set only from `BSShaderPPLightingProperty`. The material side belongs to `/audit-nifal`.
- **#5793**: the Z-up wrapper converts positions and normals through `zup_to_yup_pos`, a proper rotation, so the bitangent sign is invariant and the output is identical.
- **#5792**: the eight shape arms and four node arms in `walk/mod.rs` are semantically identical to the inline gates they replaced.
- **#5798**: the swap to core key types is a mechanical spelling change, and `canonical_key_type` keeps the `Constant` → `Const` and `XyzRotation` → `Linear` arms.
- **Pre-parse (#6104, #6119)**: `extract_guarded` wraps the extraction in `catch_unwind` and treats a panic as missing. The `ParseInputBudget` limits have binding tests. NIF-SD-01 (#6114) and NIF-SD-03 (#6116) are still open and not re-reported.
- **`decode_np_ragdoll` safety**: every read goes through bounds-checked `Option` readers. `usize` sums are built from `u32` offsets plus small constants, so they cannot overflow on 64-bit. Pointer chasing is depth 1 for compounds and wrappers, so I found no panic or unbounded allocation.
